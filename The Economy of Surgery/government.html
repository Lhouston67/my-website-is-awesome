Government policies can influence the cost, availability, and quality of healthcare. Learn how taxes, regulations, inflation, unemployment, GDP, and globalization affect the broader economy and the healthcare system.

Regulation

In the economics of surgery, regulation refers to the laws, rules, and government policies that control how surgical services are provided, how hospitals operate, and how medical professionals qualify to treat patients in certain areas of expertise.

Before people can become surgeons, they must complete their education and training and, most importantly, obtain the required licenses before they can legally practice. Hospital safety regulations cover sanitation, patient care, and infection control. There are also regulations involving health insurance and competition between healthcare providers.

Without these regulations, it would be harder to balance costs, competition, safety, and access to care. Patients could face greater risks and unfair business practices.

Tax

Taxes in surgery can include taxes paid by surgeons, hospitals, and medical businesses, as well as money collected from taxpayers to help fund public healthcare services. Taxes can affect the cost of running surgical practices and the income surgeons take home.

A hospital's tax obligations may depend on its legal structure and tax-exempt status. Depending on the location and tax rules, certain purchases for surgical care may also be subject to taxes. Taxes can help fund healthcare programs, medical training, and other public services.

Unemployment

Unemployment in surgery occurs when qualified surgical professionals cannot find suitable work, even though healthcare needs remain high. The issue often depends on where jobs are available, which specialties are needed, and whether hospitals can afford to hire more staff.

Inflation

Inflation affects the surgical industry by increasing the costs of tools, equipment, supplies, and other resources surgeons need to do their work. It can also affect salaries and potentially raise the cost of care. Hospitals must manage their budgets carefully so they can continue providing safe and effective surgical services.

GDP

GDP helps economists understand the size and importance of healthcare within the economy. Surgical care creates economic activity, provides jobs, and supports industries that manufacture medical equipment and supplies.

Globalization

Globalization connects the surgical industry across countries through trade, technology, research, patients, and healthcare workers. It can improve access to resources and sometimes lower costs, but it can also create supply-chain risks and unequal access to medical professionals around the world.
